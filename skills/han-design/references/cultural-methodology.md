# Cultural methodology

Use this reference when cultural accuracy matters more than general atmosphere.

## Classify the request

Choose one mode before selecting a theme:

| Mode | Meaning | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Historical reconstruction | The output claims to represent a specific time, place, object, or practice | Verify sources, avoid unsupported motifs, record uncertainty, and prefer object-level references |
| Historically inspired | The output borrows from documented historical material without claiming reconstruction | Name the inspiration and separate it from modern product choices |
| Contemporary Chinese-inspired | The output uses modern Chinese visual associations for a current product | Optimize usability and describe cultural references as optional inspiration |

Default to **contemporary Chinese-inspired** when the user only asks for 中国风、国风、东方美学, or a mood such as 宋韵 without requesting historical accuracy.

## Build a cultural brief

Infer or record these dimensions:

- Period: a specific period, a broad historical range, or contemporary.
- Region: name it only when the evidence and user request support it; otherwise use “not region-specific.”
- Medium: painting, calligraphy, ceramics, lacquer, textiles, metalwork, wood architecture, stone carving, print, or another documented medium.
- Setting: court, literati, religious, ritual, commercial, domestic, folk, theatrical, or public.
- Interpretation level: reconstruction, inspired, or contemporary.
- Visual intensity: subtle, balanced, or ornamental.

Do not infer ethnicity, religion, region, gender, social rank, or ritual meaning from a generic request.

## Separate evidence from design choice

For every culturally meaningful choice, distinguish:

1. **Documented reference** — supported by an object, institution, publication, or living practice.
2. **Design translation** — the modern layout, color, typography, or interaction decision derived from it.
3. **Product constraint** — accessibility, responsive behavior, information density, performance, or brand needs.

Example:

> The page uses celadon-inspired muted greens as a contemporary material reference. The accessible link color is darker than the decorative glaze color so it remains readable; it is not a historical pigment reconstruction.

## Cultural safety rules

- Do not claim that one palette, font, motif, or mood represents an entire dynasty or all Chinese culture.
- Do not combine symbols from unrelated periods, regions, religions, or social settings without a clear contemporary rationale.
- Do not use current weathering, fading, or damage as proof of an artifact's original color.
- Do not convert sacred, funerary, rank-specific, or politically sensitive imagery into decoration without checking context.
- Do not invent translations, seal-script forms, inscriptions, dates, artist names, reign marks, or provenance.
- Prefer a neutral visual solution when the source is uncertain.

## Output language

Use scoped language:

- Prefer “inspired by,” “draws from,” “references,” and “contemporary interpretation.”
- Avoid “authentic,” “traditional Chinese style,” “the Song style,” or “historically accurate” unless the evidence supports the exact claim.
- Explain important uncertainty in user-facing notes when the project is educational, archival, museum, religious, or heritage-related.

## Minimum review for sensitive work

For museums, cultural heritage, religion, ethnic culture, public history, or commercial use of living traditions:

1. Locate at least two authoritative sources when practical.
2. Prefer institutions, collection records, standards, and academic publications over mood boards.
3. Record object name, date or range, region, medium, collection, and source URL.
4. Ask for specialist review when the design makes strong claims or will represent a community publicly.
