## Qin and Han inspired

Theme: `qinhan`

- Useful references: lacquer, stone relief, tiles, seal and clerical-script associations, cloud forms, processional imagery.
- Current interpretation: dark, monumental, material-heavy, and archival.
- Do not claim: Qin and Han shared one unified visual language; all Qin-Han objects were black, ochre, and lacquer red; a fallback display font reproduces historical clerical or seal script.
- Split the treatment when accuracy matters: Qin standardization, Western/Eastern Han, Chu-influenced lacquer, tomb art, stone relief, and frontier exchange are different subjects.

## Wei, Jin, and Northern and Southern Dynasties inspired

Theme: `weijin`

- Useful references: calligraphy, literati narratives, bamboo-grove associations, early paper and silk presentation.
- Current interpretation: airy, pale, literary, and informal.
- Do not merge automatically: literati culture, Buddhist cave art, lotus, apsaras, and Northern Dynasties sculpture.
- Read object-level sources before using Buddhist imagery or presenting the theme as a historical exhibition.

## Tang inspired

Theme: `tang`

- Useful references: floral medallions, scrolling foliage, textiles, metalwork, sancai, calligraphy, murals, and cosmopolitan exchange.
- Current interpretation: ceremonial, saturated, balanced, and ornamental.
- Do not claim: Tang design was uniformly red and gold, or “prosperous grandeur” explains the entire period.
- Distinguish court, religious, funerary, urban, Silk Road, and everyday contexts.

## Song inspired

Theme: `song`

- Useful references: painting, calligraphy, ceramics, collecting culture, restrained material palettes, and fine linear structure.
- Current interpretation: modern literati minimalism.
- Do not claim: all Song visual culture was minimalist, monochrome, or based on Ru ware.
- For historical work, distinguish Northern and Southern Song, court and literati practice, urban print and commerce, religious art, architecture, and specific ceramic traditions.

## Yuan inspired

Theme: `yuan`

- Useful references: blue-and-white ceramics, painting and calligraphy, theatre, textiles, and cross-regional exchange.
- Current interpretation: high contrast, broad composition, and material richness.
- Avoid personality labels such as “rough,” “primitive,” or “uncivilized.” Describe observable media and composition instead.

## Ming inspired

Theme: `ming`

- Useful references: ceramics, furniture, print, painting, textiles, court production, literati culture, and commercial publishing.
- Current interpretation: ordered, crafted, and high-contrast.
- Do not reduce Ming culture to hierarchy or palace aesthetics. Distinguish court, literati, workshop, commercial, and folk contexts.

## Qing inspired

Theme: `qing`

- Useful references: imperial workshops, enamel, porcelain, textiles, painting, print, folk art, and exchanges with multiple regions and technologies.
- Current interpretation: layered, colorful, and ornamental.
- Do not claim Qing design was uniformly dense or without whitespace.
- Account for the period's long duration, multi-ethnic imperial context, court and non-court production, regional traditions, and foreign techniques.

## Selection rules

- Choose a medium-led cultural theme instead of a dynasty theme when the request centers on porcelain, celadon, ink painting, seals, architecture, or tea.
- Use one dynasty theme as the base. Add a second historical reference only when the project has a documented reason.
- Keep modern interaction patterns modern. A historical theme does not justify illegible text, ornamental controls, or invented ritual language.
- In educational output, state the theme's scope and the material references used.
