# Calligraphy and seals

Calligraphy is a writing practice and visual art, not a font preset. Seal engraving combines script design, composition, carving, material, and imprint.

## Calligraphy-led typography

Consider these layers:

- Script association: seal, clerical, regular, running, or cursive.
- Character form: simplified, traditional, historical, variant, or reconstructed.
- Composition: columns, line flow, density, margins, title, inscription, signature, and seal placement.
- Brush and ink: weight, speed, dry and wet variation, continuity, and rhythm.
- Product role: display heading, quotation, artwork reproduction, navigation, or body text.

Rules:

- Use calligraphic fonts for short display text, not long body copy or dense application UI.
- Do not manufacture calligraphy by applying extreme letter spacing to every heading.
- Preserve normal character spacing for running or cursive associations unless a sourced composition shows otherwise.
- Keep body text in a readable serif or sans-serif stack.
- Never label a system font fallback as a reproduction of a named master's hand.
- Use image reproduction only when rights, source, transcription, and alternative text are known.

## Inscriptions and signatures

- Verify every character and reading direction.
- Do not invent classical Chinese, dates, reign titles, artist signatures, collection seals, or ownership history.
- Separate quoted text, author, date, place, signature, and seal visually.
- Keep decorative inscriptions out of controls and critical instructions.

## Seal design

Distinguish at least:

- relief or vermilion-character treatment, where characters read as the colored imprint;
- intaglio or white-character treatment, where characters read as the uninked ground;
- name seal, studio seal, collector seal, institutional seal, or purely contemporary brand mark;
- seal face, border, character composition, carving texture, side inscription, and actual imprint.

Rules:

- Treat `.han-stamp` as a contemporary seal-inspired badge by default.
- Do not call arbitrary text in a red square “traditional seal engraving.”
- Use valid characters and verify seal-script forms with a reliable source or specialist.
- Do not imitate government, legal, institutional, certification, or artist seals in a misleading way.
- Maintain contrast and accessible labels when a seal acts as a control.
- Avoid rotation and distressed texture when they reduce legibility.

## UI translation

- Use a seal button only for a rare primary or ceremonial action.
- Use normal buttons for navigation, repeated actions, destructive actions, and dense tools.
- Provide visible text or an accessible name; a single decorative character is not always sufficient.
- Use `--han-color-accent-control` and `--han-color-on-accent` for interactive seals.
- Keep decorative seal color separate from error and destructive semantics.
